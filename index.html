MindfulFeed

Designing social media interfaces that respect user attention

Question Heuristics Prototype Decisions Limitations

Research question

How might social media feed interfaces be redesigned to support more intentional browsing and greater user control?

This is a personal portfolio project by a Computer Engineering undergraduate. It is inspired by the Digital Attention Heuristics of Monge Roffarello, De Russis and Lukoff. It is not an official university project, and it has not been tested with users.

How to read this page: the two feeds are fictional, static mockups built with HTML and CSS only. The feeds scroll inside their phone screens, but nothing is clickable and no setting, timer or loading behaviour works. Numbered pins on each mockup match the numbered annotations below it.

Design rationale: four attention heuristics

The Digital Attention Heuristics ask designers to respect attention by design, instead of leaving all the effort of self-control to users. This project focuses on four of the eight heuristics.

H1 Agency

Support users’ sense of agency, so that time online feels chosen rather than captured.

Here: the feed ends, and the user decides whether to continue.

H2 Informed Usage

Give users the information they need to decide whether to start or keep going with a session.

Here: session time, post counts, and clear content sources.

H3 Balance Goal-Directed and Ritualistic Use

Favor quality of time over quantity. The paper lists infinite scroll and autoplay among patterns to minimize.

Here: no endless loading, no autoplay, fewer extras.

H4 Customization

Let users understand and adapt the environment to their goals, values and wellbeing.

Here: a preferences preview. It is a picture, not a working setting.

  1. Streak banner and activity badge H1

    These create urgency to return each day instead of helping users decide whether a visit is worth their time.

  2. Followed and suggested posts look the same H2

    Only a tiny, low-emphasis label separates them, so users may not know why a post is shown.

  3. Autoplaying video H3 H1

    Content starts without a user decision. Autoplay is one of the patterns the heuristics suggest minimizing.

  4. “Because you watched” strip H3

    It adds more recommended content to extend a session, instead of supporting a specific goal.

  5. Endless loading and a faded next post H1 H2

    The feed has no stopping point and no session information. The faded post implies more below. Scroll to the bottom to see the message; nothing actually loads. The fade is an illustration, so its text is intentionally hard to read.

  6. No visible controls H4

    Nothing on screen explains or lets users change what they see.

  1. Session information H2

    Time so far and the reminder point are visible, so users can decide whether to continue.

  2. Followed posts are labelled H2

    A text label and a solid accent border mark accounts the user chose to follow, so the source is clear without relying on colour alone.

  3. Quiet cards H3

    There is no autoplay, streak or badge. Like counts are hidden, matching the preferences preview. The intent is to cut cues that are not needed to read a post.

  4. Clear feed end H1

    The message states that all 4 followed posts have been seen and nothing loads afterwards. This gives a natural point to stop or choose to go on.

  5. Recommendations kept separate H2 H1

    They sit after the end, under their own heading, with a dashed border and a “Recommended” label, so they are optional rather than blended in.

  6. Preferences preview H4

    It shows how users could adjust order, recommendations, reminders and counts. It is an illustration only.

Limitations

  • No user testing has been done, so this project makes no claims about effectiveness.
  • The feeds can be scrolled inside their fixed phone screens, but everything else is static. Session timing, preferences and infinite loading are non-functional: the timer, streak and “Loading more posts…” message are fixed text.
  • The Conventional Feed is a simplified, fictional example, not a model of any real product. Its extra content is an intentional difference between the two mockups.
  • Images are simple CSS illustrations, so they are less realistic than photographs.
  • It covers four of the eight heuristics, and only for a feed screen.
  • A mindful feed may not suit every goal. For example, some users may want content to keep coming. This is a design question to explore, not a settled one.
